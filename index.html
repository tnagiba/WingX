Scope: Global business jets by certification. Part 135 is on-demand charter including jet card flights. Part 91K is fractional ownership (NetJets, Flexjet, and peers). All market totals are complete through October 2026 and come from a single WingX pull dated November 2, 2026, so every month is measured the same way. WingX revises past months as late data arrives, so figures can shift slightly between refreshes. Airport counts come from an October 26 pull; October airport counts by cabin class are estimated from the first 25 days.
Key Takeaways, October 2026
1. 2026 is a solid growth year. Charter hours are up 5.4% through October. Every month from January through September was up year over year, March strongest at +12.1% and Q3 averaging +7.0%. October was -1.4% against October 2025, the largest month of last year.
2. Charter is up 5.4% year to date, fractional up 9.5%. Fractional has grown every year since 2022 and now holds 36% of combined hours. Charter is still about 3% below 2022 levels.
3. Magellan is growing faster than the market. Magellan flew 3,845 US jet departures from January through September, up 21.0% from 3,177 last year. The US charter market grew 8.3% over the same months. Magellan's share of US charter departures rose from 0.52% in 2024 to 0.55% in 2025 and 0.62% this year.
4. Super Midsize is the growth cabin. +14.2% year to date; every other class is between -2.7% and +5.7%. It trailed heavy by 13% in 2025 and by 2% this year. Challenger 300 (+29%), Challenger 350 (+22%), Citation X (+17%) are the fastest-growing types in the class.
5. Florida is the largest charter market. It leads US charter departures by 43% over California. Magellan's share is 0.76% in Florida and 0.41% in California.
6. Operator shares are moving. Wheels Up Private Jets hours are down 33% year to date. Growing fastest: Jet Excellence +220%, Baker Aviation +98%, Executive Jet Management (US) +31%. VistaJet's 3 certificates together are down 5%.

Magellan Pace vs Market

Each line is monthly departures compared with its own average month from January to September 2025, set at 100. Market is US Part 135 charter departures, Light through Heavy jets. Magellan is flown jet departures from the Salesforce warehouse.How this is calculated: both lines are monthly departure counts divided by that series' own January to September 2025 average, so 100 means a typical 2025 month and 120 means 20% above it. Market = Part 135 charter jet departures worldwide, from WingX. Magellan = flown US jet departures from the Salesforce data warehouse, one per flight leg, cancellations and test accounts removed. Indexing each series to itself removes the scale difference and shows whether Magellan is growing faster or slower than the market it operates in. Magellan data covers January to September each year; the gaps are months not yet pulled from the warehouse.
Insight
From January through September, Magellan's departures were up 21.0% on last year and the US charter market's up 8.3%. On the chart that shows as Magellan's line averaging 121 against the market's 108. Magellan's line moves more month to month because its base is small: about 427 departures a month against roughly 68k for the market. Magellan figures run through September.
As of October 31, 2026.
